# Creative Production System

## The Narrative Arc - 9 Shots
Every video ad follows this. The villain structure is what makes it convert. You are not selling a product. You are telling a story where the product is the weapon that defeats the enemy.

1. Relatable problem
2. Villain reveal
3. Villain escalation
4. Hero mechanism introduction
5. Mechanism at work
6. Product reveal
7. Transformation
8. Social proof
9. CTA with offer

The villain is never the customer. It is the thing acting on them.

BAD villain: "You're not disciplined enough about sleep."
GOOD villain: "Cheap magnesium oxide passes straight through you."

## Worked Example
Product: magnesium supplement. Angle: you are not tired, you are waking at 3am

| Shot | Scene | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bedroom, clock reads 3:04am, eyes open | "You fall asleep fine. That was never the problem." |
| 2 | Pill bottle glows, label reads Magnesium Oxide | "It's what's in the bottle you already own." |
| 3 | Capsule dissolves, passes straight through a body outline | "Oxide absorbs at 4%. The rest leaves your body." |
| 4 | Second capsule, molecules lock onto a receptor | "Bisglycinate binds to glycine. It gets through." |
| 5 | Brain waves settle from jagged to smooth | "Glycine is what quiets the nervous system at 3am." |
| 6 | Product on nightstand, clean studio light | "400mg per dose. Third-party tested." |
| 7 | Same clock reads 6:45am, sunlight, person waking rested | "Same person. Seven nights later." |
| 8 | Review cards stack up on screen | "12,000 five-star reviews." |
| 9 | Product plus offer text | "60-night guarantee. Free shipping over $50." |

## The 7 Formats
| Format | Deploy for | Funnel stage |
|---|---|---|
| Claymation | Emotional purchase drivers: supplements, skincare, baby, pet, food, gifting | TOF |
| Pixar-style 3D | Mass-market, huge TAM, where brand recall matters | TOF / MOF |
| Balloon world | Wildcard. Fatiguing creative, or markets where every ad looks identical | MOF |
| Musical / singing | Mass-market bought on frequency: beverages, snacks, personal care | TOF |
| Famous cartoon style | Children's, pet, family brands, anything bought by parents | MOF |
| UGC-style AI | Proof and objection handling. Hybrid only, never fully synthetic | MOF / BOF |
| Cinematic product film | High-ticket, $100+ AOV. Also as a 6-second brand halo bumper | BOF |

Claymation says "we're authentic." Pixar says "we're the category leader." Choose on positioning.

By budget:
- Under $5k/month on YouTube: pick ONE format, test, scale the winner
- $5k-$20k/month: run TWO, split by funnel stage
- $20k+/month: run THREE across TOF/MOF/BOF with dedicated creative for each

## Image Prompt Template (GPT Images 2.0)
Every shot prompt states: style direction, lighting setup, camera angle, character description, environment, product placement, colour palette using the brand hex codes, on-screen text with exact spelling. Output: 2K resolution, 9:16 aspect ratio.

BAD: "A person looking tired in bed, cinematic"
GOOD: "Cinematic 3D render, cool blue key light from a window at frame left, low-angle medium shot, woman mid-30s facing camera, dark bedroom, digital clock on nightstand reading 3:04, palette #1B2A4A and #E8D5B7, no on-screen text, 2K, 9:16"

## Animation Prompt Template (Seedance 2.5)
Every frame prompt states: movement style, character action, camera direction (push in, pan, static), native audio cues, duration in seconds. Output: 1080p.

## Cost
Around $3 per finished video ad. A studio charges $3,000-$5,000 and takes 3 weeks.

## 4-Week Testing Framework
| Week | Test | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 concepts, one angle each, equal budget | No changes for 7 days |
| 2 | Kill the bottom 2. Build 3 hook variants of the top 2. | Hook only, body unchanged |
| 3 | Kill losing hooks. Vary style on the winners. | One variable at a time |
| 4 | Scale the winner. Start 5 new concepts from fresh pain points. | Winner stays running |

Once a treatment wins, hold it fixed and vary only secondary elements - palette, camera angle, duration. This is how a prompt library compounds into an asset nobody can copy.

## Repurposing From Meta
Take a top-performing Meta creative. Identify the hook, the core angle, the proof, and the CTA structure. Rewrite as YouTube-native at the target length. No new production required.

## Concept Ranking And Hook Diagnostics (strategy layer)

Distilled from marketingskills hook-system.md and creative-roadmap.md (MIT, Corey Haines). The 9 shots and 7 formats above say what to make. This section says which concept to make first, how much to spend on it, and what to fix when it underperforms.

A concept is one testable hypothesis: segment x motivation x angle x format, with its evidence attached. "UGC for parents" is not a concept. "New-parent insomniacs (40+ reviews mention 3am feeds) x quiet enough not to wake the baby x claymation" is.

Rank every concept by its strongest evidence.

| Tier | Evidence | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your own account, a converting ad with the same angle and segment | Strongest. Iterate and extend. |
| 2 | Customers verbatim, recurring review or call language | Strong. Build new creative on it. |
| 3 | Competitor creative running 60+ days (presumed working) | Good. Adapt the angle, never the ad. |
| 4 | Organic engagement in the niche on the theme | Moderate. Validate cheaply first. |
| 5 | Cross-niche pattern from an adjacent category | Weak. Icebox until corroborated. |
| 6 | Team hunch, no external signal | Weakest. Low-fidelity test or drop. |

Fidelity laddering. Hunches ship low-fidelity within a day or two (statics, text-on-screen, voiceover over b-roll). Validated angles earn the full 9-shot production. Never spend a full production run on an unvalidated angle, and never test a proven angle with a throwaway static.

A hook is three components, not a line: the visual action in seconds 0-3, the first spoken line, and the caption text. They must complement, never repeat each other. If the voiceover and the caption say the same thing, a third of the hook is wasted. Statics collapse to two components (visual plus headline) under the same rule.

The diagnostic funnel. Read all four stages before declaring a winner or a loser, and change one component per iteration.

| Stage | Metric | Weak means the problem is | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop | Thumbstop, 3-second view rate | The visual action (and caption) | New visual opening, everything else unchanged |
| Stay | Hold rate, 3 to 15 seconds | The on-ramp, what follows the hook | Rework seconds 3-15, not the hook |
| Click | CTR | Desire or offer clarity mid-ad | Sharpen the promise, CTA, or proof |
| Convert | Post-click CVR | Congruence, the page does not continue the ad | Fix the landing page or the claim |

A great thumbstop is not a great ad. A clickbait opener that pulls the wrong viewers shows as high thumbstop with collapsed hold and CVR. Every hook test is also an on-ramp test; a new hook on an old body usually breaks the premise bridge, so rewrite seconds 3-15 to match each hook.

Account state decides the mix. Exploration state (nothing working): go wide, mostly net-new concepts, count a single-metric lift as a hit, iterate only on hits. Scaling state (a winner exists): go deep on the winner with visually distinct variations of the same message, keep a small exploration slice alive because winners fatigue and the next winner is rarely an iteration of the current one.
